Gerund or infinitive?

Some verbs come with a second verb attached, like a singer with a backing band. The first verb decides what the second one looks like. There are three patterns:

Gerundverb + -ingI enjoy playing the drums.
to-infinitiveverb + to + verbI want to play the drums.
Infinitive without toverb + verbI can play the drums.

A rough guide (with exceptions): verbs that look ahead, like plans, wishes, decisions and promises, often take to (want, hope, decide, promise). Verbs about experiences and feelings, things you do or imagine doing, often take -ing (enjoy, mind, miss, imagine). Modal verbs like can, must and should take the bare verb. And after a preposition you always use -ing: look forward to playing, feel like singing.

Watch out: stop and regret

On your list, stop and regret take -ing, and that's what you practise on this page. You'll sometimes see them with to as well, but then the meaning changes:

stop + -ing

no longer do something
Stop playing! The neighbours are complaining.

stop + to

stop what you're doing in order to do something else
On the way to the gig, we stopped to buy guitar strings.

regret + -ing

be sorry about something in the past
I regret selling my old guitar.

regret + to

formal, when giving bad news
We regret to inform you that tonight's concert has been cancelled.

Also note: help works with or without to (help us (to) write), and make changes with the passive: They made us practise → We were made to practise.
